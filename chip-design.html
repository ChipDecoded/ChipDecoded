What Is Chip Design?

Chip design is the engineering process of planning, developing, verifying and implementing the electronic circuits that perform specific functions inside an integrated circuit.

Understanding Chip Design

A modern semiconductor chip can contain millions or even billions of electronic components. Designing such a complex system requires multiple engineering stages and specialized tools.

Engineers begin with the required functionality and gradually convert that idea into a detailed hardware implementation that can eventually be manufactured on a semiconductor wafer.

Basic Chip Design Flow

A simplified chip design process can be represented by the following stages:

1

Specification

Engineers define what the chip needs to do, including performance, power and functional requirements.

2

Architecture

The major functional blocks and their relationships are planned at the system level.

3

RTL Design

Digital hardware behavior is described using hardware description languages such as Verilog or VHDL.

4

Verification

The design is tested using simulations and other verification techniques to identify functional problems.

5

Synthesis

RTL descriptions are converted into a gate-level representation suitable for implementation.

6

Physical Design

The circuit is implemented physically by planning placement, routing and other layout-related information.

7

Fabrication

The completed design is manufactured on semiconductor wafers using a semiconductor fabrication process.

8

Testing

Manufactured devices are tested to check whether they meet the required electrical and functional specifications.

Front-End and Back-End Design

Chip design is often discussed in terms of front-end and back-end activities.

Front-End Design: focuses mainly on functionality, architecture, RTL development and verification.

Back-End Design: focuses on turning the logical design into a physical implementation through activities such as floorplanning, placement and routing.

Digital, Analog and Mixed-Signal Design

Semiconductor design can involve different types of electronic circuits.

Digital design works primarily with discrete logic states and is used in processors, controllers and digital systems.

Analog design deals with continuously varying electrical signals and is important in areas such as amplification, sensing and power management.

Mixed-signal design combines analog and digital circuitry in the same system.

Why Is Chip Design Important?

Chip design determines many characteristics of an electronic system, including functionality, performance, power consumption, physical size and manufacturing requirements.

As electronic devices become more capable, chip designers continue to develop new architectures and implementation techniques to meet increasingly complex requirements.
