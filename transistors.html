Transistors & Semiconductor Technology

Explore the tiny electronic devices that form the basic building blocks of modern integrated circuits.

What Is a Transistor?

A transistor is a semiconductor device that can be used to control electrical current. In digital circuits, transistors are combined to implement logic functions and other circuit structures.

Modern processors, memory devices and many other integrated circuits contain very large numbers of transistors.

Why Are Transistors Important?

Transistors allow electronic circuits to perform switching, amplification and other forms of signal control.

In digital electronics, controlled switching between electrical states allows transistors to form logic gates and more complex digital systems.

Common Transistor Technologies

MOSFET

The Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor is a key device used in modern integrated circuits. MOSFETs can act as controlled electronic switches and are fundamental to CMOS digital logic.

CMOS

CMOS, or Complementary Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor, is a widely used circuit technology that combines complementary transistor types to implement digital logic with low static power consumption.

FinFET

FinFET is a three-dimensional transistor structure in which the conducting channel is formed in a fin-like structure. It was developed to provide better control of the channel as transistor dimensions became smaller.

Gate-All-Around

Gate-All-Around, often abbreviated GAA, is a transistor architecture in which the gate surrounds the channel more completely. This structure can provide strong electrostatic control of the channel.

From Planar Transistors to 3D Structures

As semiconductor technology advanced, transistor structures evolved to improve control of the conducting channel and support continued scaling.

Planar transistor: The channel is formed primarily along a relatively flat surface.

FinFET: The channel is formed in a raised fin-like structure.

GAA: The gate surrounds the channel more extensively, providing stronger control of the channel.

Transistors Inside a Digital Chip

A single transistor is only a small building block. Chip designers combine very large numbers of transistors to create logic gates, arithmetic units, memory arrays, processors and other functional blocks.

These blocks are then connected to create the larger architecture of an integrated circuit.

What Is a Process Node?

A semiconductor process node is a term used to describe a particular generation of semiconductor manufacturing technology. Modern node names are not simply a direct measurement of one physical transistor dimension.

Different process generations can provide changes in transistor structures, density, performance, power characteristics and manufacturing technology.

Why Transistor Technology Keeps Evolving

Semiconductor engineers continuously work to improve transistor performance, power efficiency, density and reliability while dealing with physical and manufacturing challenges.

New transistor architectures, materials, manufacturing techniques and packaging technologies all contribute to the development of modern semiconductor systems.
