How Are Chips Made?

From a piece of silicon to a highly complex semiconductor device, chip manufacturing involves many carefully controlled steps.

What Is Chip Manufacturing?

Chip manufacturing is the process of turning a semiconductor design into a physical electronic device.

Modern chips contain extremely large numbers of transistors and other structures. These structures are created on semiconductor wafers using processes that build, remove, and modify very thin layers of material.

Manufacturing takes place in specialized facilities called semiconductor fabrication plants, commonly known as fabs.

Main Steps in Chip Manufacturing

STEP 01

Silicon

Silicon is widely used as the semiconductor material for integrated circuits. It is processed into a highly pure form before being used to make wafers.

STEP 02

Wafer Preparation

The processed silicon is formed into thin, flat circular wafers. The wafer surface is polished to provide a smooth foundation for manufacturing.

STEP 03

Layer Formation

Very thin layers of different materials can be formed on the wafer. These layers help create the structures required for transistors and other circuit elements.

STEP 04

Photolithography

Photolithography is used to transfer carefully designed patterns onto the wafer. Light is used with a photosensitive material called photoresist to define selected areas.

STEP 05

Etching

Etching removes selected portions of a material layer. This helps create the physical patterns required by the circuit design.

STEP 06

Doping

Doping changes the electrical properties of selected regions of semiconductor material by introducing controlled amounts of specific impurities.

STEP 07

Metal Interconnects

Conductive structures are created to connect different components of the integrated circuit. These connections allow electrical signals to travel through the chip.

STEP 08

Packaging

After wafer processing and separation, individual dies can be placed into packages that provide physical protection and electrical connections to the outside world.

STEP 09

Testing

Semiconductor devices are tested to check their electrical behavior and performance against specified requirements.

Understanding Photolithography

Photolithography is one of the important patterning techniques used during semiconductor manufacturing.

A layer of photoresist is applied to the wafer. A pattern is then transferred using controlled exposure to light. After development, selected areas of the photoresist remain or are removed depending on the process.

The resulting pattern can be used during later processing steps such as etching or material modification.

What Is Etching?

Etching is a process used to selectively remove material from the wafer.

Two broad categories are commonly discussed: wet etching, which uses liquid chemicals, and dry etching, which uses controlled gases or plasma-based processes.

The choice of etching technique depends on the material, structure, and manufacturing requirements.

What Is Doping?

Pure semiconductor material does not always have the electrical properties required for a particular circuit structure. Doping is used to modify those properties.

  • Specific impurity atoms are introduced into selected regions.
  • The electrical behavior of the semiconductor can be controlled.
  • Doping is important in creating semiconductor device structures.
  • Ion implantation is one widely used technique for introducing dopants.

Why Are Multiple Layers Needed?

A modern integrated circuit is not created from a single material layer. Different layers are used to form semiconductor regions, insulating structures, conductive connections, and other features.

Manufacturing repeatedly performs processes such as deposition, patterning, etching, cleaning, and material modification to build the required three-dimensional structure.

Important: Semiconductor manufacturing involves many more detailed process steps than the simplified flow shown on this page. The exact sequence varies with the technology and device being manufactured.

From Wafer to Individual Chips

Many individual chip designs can be manufactured across a single semiconductor wafer.

After the wafer-level manufacturing processes are completed, the wafer can be tested and separated into individual pieces called dies.

These dies can then go through packaging and additional testing before becoming usable semiconductor products.

Why Chip Manufacturing Is Challenging

Extreme Precision

Modern semiconductor structures can be extremely small, requiring highly controlled manufacturing processes.

Clean Environment

Tiny particles can interfere with manufacturing, so chip fabrication requires carefully controlled cleanroom environments.

Process Control

Temperature, pressure, chemicals, materials, timing, and other parameters must be carefully controlled.

Many Process Steps

A modern chip can require a large number of interconnected manufacturing operations before it becomes a finished device.

Chip Manufacturing in Simple Words

Think of chip manufacturing as building a very complex structure layer by layer on a silicon wafer.

Some processes add material, some remove material, and others change the electrical properties of selected regions. Repeating these operations creates the tiny structures and connections that form an integrated circuit.
